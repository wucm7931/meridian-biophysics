>
> **Column legend**:
> - R = |RMP| / 100 (dimensionless ionic order; 100 mV = the theoretical hyperpolarization limit of mammalian cells)
> - Type: depolarized = cancer R < normal R (**90.9%, 30/33**); hyperpolarized = cancer R > normal R (3.0%, 1/33); tied = cancer R ≈ normal R (6.1%, 2/33). Sign test: 30/31 non-tied pairs, two-sided exact binomial test **p = 3.0×10⁻⁸** (recomputed)
> - Pairing: same-study = normal and cancer measured in the same paper under the same conditions; cross-study = from different papers; cross-species = different species
> - Provenance: Level-1 = same-study pairing + PMID-verified consistency; Level-2 = PMID available but individual values pending confirmation; Level-3 = source of the normal or cancer value uncertain
> - ⚠️ rows are used in the main-text statistics, but their values have known issues (see Notes 1–12)
> - Valid pairs 33 (37 rows in the historical version − HaCaT reference row − MTC − A549 − ROS-17/2.8; the MTC/A549/ROS exclusion reasons are in Notes 8/9/11). Provenance grading: Level-1 14 / Level-2 19 / Level-3 1 (HaCaT reference row)
> - **Companion CSV (`data/paired_rmp_data.csv`) column dictionary**: `cell_type`=cell type; `normal_rmp`/`cancer_rmp`=normal/cancer RMP absolute value (mV); `cell_line`=cancer cell line; `method`=measurement method; `quality`=provenance grade (Level-1/2/3, same as the provenance column in this table); `pmid`=source PMID (empty = cross-study without a single PMID); `source`=source publication; `notes`=pair-by-pair notes. The `asr` column is a residual variable from the V7-version withdrawn ASR regression (log ASR), not used in this table's statistics, to be deleted at finalization.

## Table notes

### Note 1: Normal value of the mammary epithelium
In Data 1 the normal mammary RMP = 45 mV is used as the reference for the MCF-7 four subpopulations (rows 20–23) and MDA-MB-231 (row 24). This value takes a compromise of two sources: (i) Blaug et al., 2001 (PMID 11443063), the apical membrane potential of the 31EG4 mouse mammary-epithelial cell line V_A = −40.7 mV (whole-cell about −46.6 mV, an immortalized cell line, intracellular microelectrode); (ii) Berga and Neville, 1984, lactating-mouse in-vivo mammary epithelium −49±2 mV (microelectrode, cited via Blaug 2001). The 45 mV is a conservative value rounded near the lower bound of the two sources (−46.6 and −49 mV) — taking the more depolarized end, so that the depolarization amplitude is under-estimated rather than over-estimated. ⚠️ Both sources are mouse, and 31EG4 is immortalized — normal human primary mammary-epithelium RMP data are extremely scarce in the literature. The choice of this value has a systematic influence on the R values of the MCF-7 subpopulations, but whether −40.7 or −49 is taken, the MCF-7 subpopulations (R_C 0.09–0.40) and MDA-MB-231 (R_C ≈0.30) are all still clearly depolarized, not changing the depolarized-type classification. The original Note 1 statement "Marino 1994 −40~−58 midpoint = 45" had two errors — the midpoint is in fact −49, and Marino 1994 has no evidence file — and has been corrected to Blaug 2001.

### Note 2: Normal value of the prostate epithelium and the LNCaP cancer value
The normal prostate-epithelium RMP takes the human PNT2-C2 value −36±1.9 mV (Mycielska and Djamgoz, 2004, PMID 15254154 [36]; ⚠️ the originally used rat-prostate microelectrode Szatkowski et al., 2000 median −40 mV was a conservative lower bound, PMID 11119104 [30]; the literature range is −40~−55 mV, and PNT2-C2 was adopted for same-species matching). The LNCaP (row 27) cancer value takes Gutiérrez et al., 1999 (PMID 10226152, *J Physiol* 517(Pt 1):95–107) [15] perforated-patch measured: "a stable membrane potential of −39.5 ± 2 mV" (n=27). LNCaP −39.5 vs. normal −36 → R_C 0.40 and R_N 0.36 differ by only 0.04, **judged "tied"** (not counted in the hyperpolarized type; ⚠️ threefold reasoning: first, the difference is only 3.5 mV, near the ±2 mV measurement-error edge; second, it is caused by the choice of the human normal value — if the rat −40 value were retained, it would also be "tied"; third, and more fundamentally, LNCaP (Gutiérrez 1999 perforated patch) and PNT2-C2 (Mycielska 2004 whole-cell patch) are not the same solutions or the same batch of experiments, being cross-laboratory, cross-year, and different in method, so the 3.5 mV difference between −39.5 and −36 is too small for a hyperpolarized/depolarized comparison).

⚠️ The original PC-3 row **has been deleted**: its annotated source Laniado et al., 2001 (PMID 11241548) contains no RMP value in the abstract, and the full text cannot be obtained. The PC-3 parental-strain RMP has, to date, no clean direct current-clamp/perforated-patch measured value — its lineage literature (Maroto et al., 2012, PMID 22874798, the same Hamill laboratory) explains why it is hard to measure: the PC-3 cell-membrane stiffness is about 5 times that of LNCaP (AFM Young's modulus), the tight seal requires ≥40 mmHg vs. 5 mmHg of stronger and longer negative pressure, and the seal resistance is only ~1 GΩ vs. ~10 GΩ, so only a cell-attached indirect estimate of −30~−50 mV (uncorrected for liquid-junction potential) can be given, overlapping with the normal −36~−40 and unable to support the "depolarized" classification. Note: its liver-metastasis subline PC-3M has a direct whole-cell measured value −52.3±6.1 mV (Mycielska et al., 2005, PMID 15611019 [37]), but PC-3M is another subline and cannot directly replace the PC-3 parental strain; referring to the lineage-literature view, PC-3 (parental strain/sublines) may be a hyperpolarized-type cancer relative to the normal prostate epithelium, but because the parental strain lacks a clean direct single value, it is not included in this table.

### Note 3: QT-35 / Srivastava 2021 data correction
Srivastava et al., 2021 Supplementary Table S1 (S23) recorded the QT-35 fibrosarcoma RMP as −40.1 mV (the same as the normal quail fibroblast −40.1 mV, ratio=1.00). After checking against the original literature Binggeli and Weinstein, 1985 (PMID 3965134), the correct QT-35 RMP under high-density/high-contact conditions is −20.7 mV. This table adopts the corrected original-paper value. This correction does not affect the main conclusion of Srivastava et al. (cancer-cell depolarization), and only corrects this specific data point.

### Note 4: HT-29 colon-cancer hyperpolarized-type pairing
In Data 1 row 26, the NCM460 RMP = 45 mV and HT-29 RMP = 64 mV come from Sobradillo et al., 2014 (PMID 25143380, PMC4200238). The PMC full text states explicitly: "The estimated resting membrane potential for HT29 cells was −64 ± 2 mV (n = 33) and for NCM460 cells was −45 ± 4 mV (n = 22)" — planar patch clamp (Port-a-Patch, current-clamp). The cancer-cell hyperpolarization (ratio = 1.42) is driven by Ca²⁺ remodeling: TRPC1↑/STIM2↓ (reciprocal transfer) + ORAI1↑/STIM1↑ → SOCE↑ (ICRAC + ISOC mixed current) → Ca²⁺↑ → more negative membrane potential → hyperpolarization (the original paper only states the "more negative membrane potential" correlation and does not directly identify the channel; KCa3.1 activation is a downstream inference, with the direct literature in Tajima, 2006) — a typical mechanism of the hyperpolarized type (Ca²⁺-driven type). Room-temperature (20±2°C) recording may cause systematic under-estimation of the absolute value — but the pairing ratio is relatively reliable. The RMP of HT-29 is highly dependent on the differentiation/subclone state — independent literature reports a −50~−60 mV range — and Sobradillo 2014's −64 mV is one of the most polarized values reported. **Baseline sensitivity (key)**: this table's HT-29 pairing uses NCM460 (−45 mV) as the normal reference, so R_C/R_N = 0.64/0.45 = 1.42, judged hyperpolarized. But NCM460 is an immortalized normal colonic-epithelial cell line, and its −45 mV is clearly shallower than the −60 mV colonic-epithelium consensus value used in this table's T84 row (row 32) (Note 6). If the −60 mV consensus baseline is adopted instead, then ΔR = 0.64 − 0.60 = +0.04, falling into the tied interval (ΔR ≤ 0.05 judged tied, see main text Section III C) — the status of HT-29 as the "only confirmed hyperpolarized example" therefore depends on the choice of the normal baseline, and is a baseline-sensitive judgment.

### Note 5: Liver-cancer cell lines (Sun et al., 2003)
Sun et al., 2003 (PMID 12799308), in the Results ("Cellular PDs") of the main text, reports the resting non-malignant human hepatocyte PD = −25.1±1.5 mV, and the proliferative state −20.1±1.6 mV. All four malignant cell lines are clearly depolarized: Chang −7.5±1.0, HepG2 −9.8±0.5, HuH-7 −4.2±0.3, PLC/PRF/5 −3.2±0.4 mV (P<0.0001 vs. resting normal). The values used in Data 1 are exactly consistent with the main text. The "approximately −35 mV (39)" in the abstract cites previous literature — not Sun 2003's own measured value. The fluorescent dye DiBAC₄(3) is an indirect optical method — its absolute-value precision is lower than electrophysiology — but the directional conclusion is robust. The normal-hepatocyte RMP anchor value differs markedly across methods: the patch-clamp gold standard reports −42±7 mV (Sawanobori 1989, PMID 2544611, rat) and −38.6±0.3 mV (Saito 1992 TPP⁺ method, PMID 1329961), and room-temperature perforated patch can be as low as −4~−15 mV (Sato 2008, PMID 19079290, rat). This table's −25.1 mV is Sun 2003's same-study paired value, retained to maintain same-study pairing; but its absolute-value precision is lower than patch clamp, and PLC/PRF/5 (−3.2 mV, R=0.03) and HuH-7 (−4.2 mV, R=0.04) fall below the F-ratchet survival lower bound R≈0.05, possibly due to dye-method under-estimation (consistent with the "measurement-artifact" clause of main-text Prediction 1).

### Note 6: Provenance grading and the remaining consensus values
Among the 33 valid pairs in this table, except for a few normal reference values, all sources have been traced to a single verifiable PMID (see references) — the provenance grading is now Level-1 14 rows, Level-2 19 rows, Level-3 0 rows (within the valid pairs). What remain marked "⚠️ consensus value" are only a few normal reference values (T cell −60, plasma cell −55, colonic epithelium −60), which come from the field-consensus range (Kiefer 1980 / Rink 1980 / Verheugen 1995, etc.) rather than a single traceable PMID — this is due to the scarcity of primary normal-tissue-cell RMP data in the literature, not a loss of source. The original v7-table statement "Level-3 rows lack a single traceable PMID" is outdated — the evidence directory `data/_evidence/rmp/` has completed the trace-back writing.

### Note 7: MDA-MB-231 RMP method difference
There is a method difference in the reported MDA-MB-231 RMP values: Quicke et al., 2022 (voltage-sensitive-dye imaging) about −30 mV (R≈0.30), Payne et al., 2021 (EBioMedicine) −19.5 mV (R≈0.20). This table's row 24 adopts Quicke 2022 ~−30 mV (with a local evidence file [H]); Payne 2021 −19.5 mV has no local evidence file and is kept as an alternative measurement. Both values are far lower than the normal mammary-epithelium reference value 45 mV, and the depolarized classification is robust. Note: the Payne 2021 "K⁺-channel overexpression → hyperpolarization" described in Data 3's H3 subtype is an engineered overexpression state, not the MDA-MB-231 native baseline (the native is depolarized, see above).

### Note 8: Exclusion of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
The original medullary-thyroid-carcinoma (MTC) row (normal 36.6 → cancer 50.1, judged "hyperpolarized") **has been excluded from the main statistics**. Its "hyperpolarization" is a **cross-lineage artifact**: the normal value 36.6 mV comes from the hamster thyroid **follicular** epithelium (Jamakosmanovic and Loewenstein, 1968 [5]), while the cancer value 50.1 mV comes from the human thyroid **parafollicular C cell** (neuroendocrine origin, Sand et al., 1986 [14]). The two are not the same cell lineage; the C cell developmentally retains a more polarized neuroendocrine electrical phenotype, and its 50.1 > 36.6 does not constitute evidence of "cancerous hyperpolarization" — the decisive exclusion reason is **cross-lineage** (the direction is flipped by the lineage electrical phenotype), with cross-species only a secondary confound. ⚠️ **Cross-species in itself does not constitute an exclusion reason**: a cross-species pairing within the same lineage is retained as long as ΔR far exceeds the species-confounding magnitude and the direction can still be attributed to malignant transformation (e.g., Row 14, see Note 12). Hence it is removed from the main statistics.

### Note 9: Exclusion of A549 alveolar cancer
The original A549 row (human alveolar epithelium 50 → A549 10, judged "depolarized") **has been excluded from the main statistics**. The normal alveolar-epithelium RMP reference value 50 mV has no single traceable source and contradicts the measurements — Shlyonsky et al., 2008 (PMID 18276797) measured the rat type-II alveolar epithelial (AT2) RMP = −4.4±1.9 mV (AT2 is naturally depolarized, with no reliable "normal" baseline). A549 itself originates from the AT2 lineage, and its −10 mV reflects the AT2's natural depolarization ceiling, not "cancerous depolarization". Hence this row is removed from the main statistics, avoiding the fabrication of false depolarization evidence with an erroneous normal baseline.

### Note 10: IGR1 melanoma KCa bistability

The IGR1 cancer value ~−30 mV in Row 25 adopts the KCa closed state (unstimulated resting state) of Nilius 1990. IGR1 is a KCa-bistable cell: when intracellular Ca²⁺ rises (e.g., A23187) or HIF-1α is activated, KCa opens → hyperpolarization to ~−77 mV, exceeding the normal melanocyte −70.3 mV (Xie 2018). This hyperpolarization dynamic is in Data 3 (H1→H4 dynamic cases), and does not change this row's "depolarized-type" classification — this row takes the unstimulated resting state as the cancer value.

### Note 11: Exclusion of ROS-17/2.8 osteosarcoma

The original Row 33 (human osteoblast −60 → rat ROS-17/2.8 −42, judged "depolarized") **has been excluded from the main statistics**. Its "depolarization" is an **attribution failure under the triple confound of cross-study + cross-species + small Δ**: the normal value −60 mV comes from Pangalos et al., 2011 [24] (human primary osteoblast, 2011), the cancer value −42 mV from Dixon and Aubin, 1984 [25] (rat ROS-17/2.8 osteosarcoma line, 1984), and the ΔRMP is only 18 mV — while the magnitudes of the human/rat species difference, the 2011/1984 era difference, and the primary/cell-line method difference can together cover 18 mV, so the direction cannot be attributed to malignant transformation. Moreover, this pairing violates the "human-first" inclusion criterion: the human osteosarcoma line MG-63 has a clean measured value, yet the rat ROS was adopted. More critically, there is a **same-species, same-study counter-evidence**: Pangalos et al., 2011, in the same study and same method, measured human primary osteoblasts and the human osteosarcoma line MG-63, and both are **−60 mV → tied** — the cleanest same-species osteosarcoma evidence does not support "depolarization". Hence this row is removed, avoiding the fabrication of false depolarization evidence with a cross-species difference (same issue as Notes 8/9).

### Note 12: Cross-species robustness of Row 14 skeletal muscle → rhabdomyosarcoma (‡)

Row 14 (rat skeletal muscle −95.6 → mouse rhabdomyosarcoma −16.3, Balitsky et al., 1964 [8]) is a **same-study cross-species** pairing (normal side rat, cancer side mouse). Its "depolarized" direction is **not threatened by the species difference**: even if the normal side's rat −95.6 mV were replaced by the mouse skeletal-muscle value — mouse skeletal muscle ≈ −75~−80 mV (Selvin et al., 2015 [38]; FDB single fiber 37°C steady-state −75 mV, EDL/soleus −80 mV) — the ΔRMP still reaches ~64 mV, with the direction unchanged. The cross-species difference (~15 mV) is far smaller than Δ = 79 mV, so this row's "depolarization" attributable to malignant transformation (skeletal muscle losing excitability → RMP collapse) holds. This row's Δ is slightly over-estimated, but its direction and extreme amplitude (the largest in the whole table) are robust.
